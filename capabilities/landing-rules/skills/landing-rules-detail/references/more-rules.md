# The landing rules: who does the work, records, changes

## Who does the work, and the review every result gets
- The lead (or a specialist, for its area) specifies each arm in the locked protocol;
  an analyst runs it and owns its run record. Neither checks its own work.
- The analyst iterates with fresh study reviewers, each a new instance and its OATS
  child, until a round passes the head: at least one round, always, never the author
  reading its own work. The loop's cap is in the analyst's review loop.
- At the converged head, the lead sends the independent check and the leakage and
  selection review the protocol names, each a fresh study reviewer on another model, and
  verifies the run record once: `VERIFIED at <head>`. It runs no review rounds itself.
- One reviewer, one report: a study reviewer sends its verdict once and retires itself.
  A check that needs a second read gets a fresh reviewer.

Why: the one who ran an arm holds the context to answer its reviews, and a fresh reader
catches what its author can't.

## Records
Where your briefing says they are kept:
- **A study's record:** its status at the top, in words a person can read cold: what it
  is, where it stands, who is on it (instance and seat), what waits on whom. It also
  holds what your tasks layer's items never do: the arms and their analysts, the
  specialists and their areas, the interfaces agreed between them, the probes and what
  each found, and each review's verdict.
- **A finding outside your work:** one record, sent to its area's owner.

## After a change to these rules
Whoever wrote the change messages each standing instance (the dispatchers and the record
stewards) and the lead of each open study (the study records name them), with the change
at its commit. Each lead tells its running children of a change that bears on their
briefs (the sources they may use, the hosts, an instrument's bounds, say), at that
commit: its specialists, analysts, study reviewers and helpers; an analyst passes it on
to any study reviewer it still runs. Each recipient records the change, at its commit, in
its state file (a lead on its study's record too), and from then on reads these rules
with the changes it recorded, compaction or not.
